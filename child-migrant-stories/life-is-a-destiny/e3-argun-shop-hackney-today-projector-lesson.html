Read · A Hackney shop on the move

1 Argun Printers & Stationers is a family-run printing and stationery shop in Hackney. It has been open since 1980. For more than 30 years, it was in a railway arch at 344 Mare Street, near Hackney Central station. People go there to print business cards, posters and wedding invitations, and to buy pens, notebooks and art materials. They can also copy, scan and laminate documents.

2 In 2025, The Arch Company, which owns the railway arches, asked the shop to leave. The company wants to build a new development in the arches. According to the owner of the shop, Farukh Adia, the plans include pubs, clubs, restaurants and bars.

3 The company offered the shop a new place on Morning Lane. It was less than five minutes’ walk away, and the rent was lower. But Mr Adia was worried. Most of the shops on that street were closed, and not many people walked past. He said the shop would lose about half of its customers if it moved there.

4 Customers started a petition called “Support Argun”. By January 2026, more than 2,600 people had signed it. Many people said the staff were very kind and the shop sold things that were hard to find anywhere else. Some said Hackney had very few printers but already had enough bars.

5 In 2026, the shop moved – but not to Morning Lane. Its new home is 20 Amhurst Road, opposite Hackney Central Overground station. It is open from Monday to Saturday, 9am to 6pm.

Grammar: the second conditional – a real story

In 2025, Mr Adia was thinking about Morning Lane. He said the shop would lose half its customers if it moved there.

Now it’s your turn – imagine: If my favourite shop closed, I would sign a petition.

If + past simple,   would + verb

If my favourite shop closed, I would sign a petition.

I would sign a petition if my favourite shop closed.

An imaginary situation, now or in the future: my favourite shop is not closing.

Ask: “Is your favourite shop closing?” (No.) “So is this real or imaginary?” (Imaginary.) Mr Adia’s sentence was imaginary in 2025, when he was deciding – keep the practice in the present. Recall Alara’s sentence from the film lesson: If my house was being bombed, I would take my phone. Drill the sentence both ways. Learners look at: the projector. 10 minutes.

Task 3 · Practice

Complete with the verbs in brackets.

  1. If Argun ___ (move) again, its customers ___ (follow) it.
    If Argun moved again, its customers would follow it.
  2. If there ___ (be) more shoppers on Morning Lane, more shops ___ (open) there.
    If there were / was more shoppers on Morning Lane, more shops would open there.
  3. If Hackney ___ (lose) Argun, it ___ (have) even fewer printers.
    If Hackney lost Argun, it would have even fewer printers.
  4. I ___ (go) to Argun if I ___ (need) wedding invitations.
    I would go to Argun if I needed wedding invitations.
  5. If my favourite shop ___ (have) to close, I ___ (sign) a petition.
    If my favourite shop had to close, I would sign a petition.
  6. If the rent ___ (be) lower, more small shops ___ (stay) on Mare Street.
    If the rent was / were lower, more small shops would stay on Mare Street.
  7. What ___ you ___ (do) if you ___ (be) the owner?
    What would you do if you were / was the owner?

Learners write on worksheet Task 3 (10 minutes). Check: a learner reads the full sentence, then click. Number 4 has if in the middle – no comma. Learners look at: worksheet Task 3, then the projector. 15 minutes.

Write · An email to the council

Model – a different subject. Borrow the yellow expressions.

To: the local council    Subject: A crossing on Well Street

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing about the road outside my children’s school on Well Street. I have lived on Well Street for six years.

The road is very busy and the cars drive fast. There is no crossing near the school. If there were a crossing, my children would be much safer. I would also feel happier.

Please put a crossing outside the school. I hope you can do this soon.

Yours faithfully,
Amina Hassan

Task 5 · Your email

Imagine your favourite local shop, café or market has to close. Write an email to the council (60–80 words).

OpenDear Sir or Madam,
Paragraph 1Why are you writing? I am writing about …
Paragraph 2Why is the place important? If it closed, I would …
Paragraph 3What do you want? Please … / I hope you can …
CloseYours faithfully, + your full name

Dear Sir or Madam → Yours faithfully · Dear Mr Khan / Ms Lee → Yours sincerely · a capital letter after Dear · a comma after the opening and the closing.

The model is about something else (a road crossing), so learners borrow its structure and the yellow expressions, not its ideas. Read it together; point out the subject line, the opening and closing with their commas, and one job for each paragraph. Ask: “Do we know the name of the person?” (No – so Dear Sir or Madam and Yours faithfully.) Learners write on worksheet Task 5 (15 minutes); monitor for the opening and closing and for would + verb. Fast finishers swap and check each other’s email against the table. 3 learners read theirs aloud. Learners look at: the projector, then worksheet Task 5. 20 minutes.
