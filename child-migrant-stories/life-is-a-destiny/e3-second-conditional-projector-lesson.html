Grammar: the second conditional

Alara: “If my house was being bombed, I would take my phone.”

If + past simple,   would + verb

If I had to leave, I would take my phone.

I would take my phone if I had to leave.

We use it for imaginary or unlikely situations – now or in the future.

When if comes first, we use a comma ( , ).

Point to Alara’s sentence. Ask: “Is her house being bombed now?” (No – imaginary.) Show the form. Drill: If I had to leave, I would take my phone. Then swap the halves – no comma. Learners look at: the projector. 10 minutes.
WHOLE CLASS – eyes on the board

Tips

I would → I’d

would not → wouldn’t

If I was / If I were – both are OK.

✗ I would took   ✓ I would take

✗ If I would have   ✓ If I had

Question: What would you take if you had to leave?

Drill I’d and wouldn’t (back-chain: “take my phone … I’d take my phone”). Point out the two mistakes – they are the ones learners make in Task 5. Drill the question for the mingle later. Learners look at: the projector. 5 minutes.

Task 5 · Practice

Part A · Word order

  1. take / my phone / I / would
    I would take my phone.
  2. had to leave, / If / I / my photos / would / I / save
    If I had to leave, I would save my photos.
  3. you / What / take / would / ?
    What would you take?

Part B · Complete

  1. If my home ___ (be) in danger, I ___ (take) my phone.
    If my home was / were in danger, I would take my phone.
  2. If I ___ (have) only one bag, I ___ (pack) my photos.
    If I had only one bag, I would pack my photos.
  3. If we ___ (leave) quickly, we ___ (not take) the TV.
    If we left quickly, we wouldn’t take the TV.
  4. Alara ___ (call) her family if she ___ (have) her phone.
    Alara would call her family if she had her phone.
  5. If I ___ (can) save one thing, I ___ (save) my mum’s ring.
    If I could save one thing, I would save my mum’s ring.
  6. What ___ you ___ (take) if you ___ (have) to leave?
    What would you take if you had to leave?

E2: Part A and Part B 1–3. E3: all. 15 minutes on the worksheet, then check: a learner reads the full sentence, then click. Part A b) can also be: I would save my photos if I had to leave. Learners look at: worksheet Task 5, then the projector. 20 minutes.
