Task B · Read the advice page and answer – the point counts only if your first click is right

The questions and answers use different words from the text. Find the part of the text with the same meaning.

Ask Aisha – advice for readers

1 Every week readers send us a problem. Aisha, our advice writer, answers.

2 Dear Aisha, I live in a flat in Dalston. My neighbour plays loud music every night after 11pm. I start work at 6am, so I cannot sleep. I do not want to make trouble. What should I do? – Sam

3 Dear Sam, talk to your neighbour first. Knock on the door and explain that you start work early. If you are friendly, your neighbour will probably listen. If the noise continues, write to your landlord. The landlord will visit the flat if three neighbours complain, so ask the other people in your building to write too. Unless the problem is very serious, do not call the police.

4 Dear Aisha, I want to learn to drive, but one lesson costs £40. I have a part-time job and I also look after my little sister, so I do not have much money or time. Will I ever pass my test? – Dina

5 Dear Dina, yes, you will! Start by getting your provisional licence (the licence for learner drivers), because you cannot practise unless you have one. If you practise with a family member, you will need fewer lessons with a teacher. Also, make a plan. If you save £20 every week, you will have £80 after four weeks, and that will pay for two lessons. Be patient – if you keep trying, you will pass.

Learners’ code for page 3: 0000 – say it or write it on the board when the class moves on. SAY: “Page 3. First read the title and the first sentence of each paragraph – our exam routine. Then read the whole text once. Then answer the ten questions. The words in the questions are different from the words in the text. 20 minutes.” DO: do not explain words yet; ‘provisional licence’ is explained in the text. Fast finishers: find the six if / unless sentences in the text. CHECK: Show next answer one per click; for each, a learner reads the words in the text that prove it. Answers: 1 to help people who write in with a difficulty 2 The person next door is noisy late at night. 3 go and speak to the person next door 4 Three people living there must report the problem. 5 The neighbour will most likely take notice. 6 only if things get really bad 7 She is short of money and free time. 8 a special licence for people who are learning 9 She will not have to pay for so many lessons. 10 two Learners look at: page 3 on their own phone.

Task C · Condition or result?

The condition is the “if” part: what might happen. The result is the “will” part: what happens next. Look at the advice page on page 3 if you need to.

Learners’ code for page 4: 0000 – say it or write it on the board when the class moves on. SAY: “Page 4. Each piece is half of a sentence from Aisha’s advice. Is it the condition – the if part – or the result – the will part? You can go back to page 3.” MODEL number 1: “three neighbours complain – that is what might happen: condition.” DO: pairs may talk. CHECK: Show next answer one per click; ASK “What is the other half?” (e.g. 1: the landlord will visit the flat). SAY: “In the exam, questions like ‘What will happen if …?’ ask for the result – look for will.” Answers: 1 condition 2 result 3 result 4 condition 5 condition 6 result 7 condition 8 result 9 result 10 condition. Eastfield Community Library – borrowing rules

You can borrow six books for three weeks. You need a library card. Unless you have a card, you cannot borrow books.

If you return a book late, you will pay 20p a day. If you lose a book, you will pay for a new one.

You can renew a book online if nobody else is waiting for it. If you renew a book, you will have three more weeks.

The library is closed on Sundays, but you can return books through the box in the wall.

IN YOUR NOTEBOOK – write, then swap

Task E · Give advice

Choose one problem

  1. “I am always tired in class.”
  2. “My phone bill is too high.”
  3. “I want a job, but my English is not good enough yet.”
  4. “My friend never answers my messages.”

Write four sentences of advice in your notebook.

Use

If you …, you will …

Unless you …, you will …

Use if three times and unless once.

Check before you swap: capital letter · full stop · comma after the if part · no will after if.

Swap notebooks with your partner. Read their advice and tell them one thing that is good: That is good advice because …

Learners’ code for page 6: 0000 – say it or write it on the board when the class moves on. SAY: “Page 6. Look at the board. Choose one problem and give advice – four sentences in your notebook. Use if three times and unless once. Then swap with your partner and say one thing that is good.” DO: walk round; point at a wrong form and ask “Condition or result? Which verb form after if?” – do not correct it for them. Collect two good pieces of advice to read aloud on the next page. If short of time: three sentences, no swap. Learners look at: their notebook, then their partner.

Task F · Find the mistake

One mistake in each sentence. Tell your partner – then tell the class.

  1. If it rain tomorrow, we will stay at home.
    If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.
  2. If you study hard, you would pass the test.
    If you study hard, you will pass the test.
  3. Unless you do not hurry, you will miss the train.
    Unless you hurry, you will miss the train. (unless = if … not)
  4. She will phone you if she will have time.
    She will phone you if she has time.
  5. If the shop is closed, we go to the market tomorrow.
    If the shop is closed, we will go to the market tomorrow.
  6. I will not go to the party unless you will come.
    I will not go to the party unless you come.

Learners’ code for page 7: 0000 – say it or write it on the board when the class moves on. SAY: “Eyes on the board. Each sentence has one mistake. Number 1 – talk to your partner for 20 seconds.” DO: a learner says the correct sentence; THEN click (one per click). Read two good pieces of advice aloud. Answers: 1 rains 2 will pass 3 Unless you hurry 4 if she has time 5 we will go 6 unless you come. Learners look at: the projector.
