Task D · Cross Road: what will happen if …?

  1. If a market opens on Cross Road, the buses will be late.
  2. If the buses are late, people will arrive late at work.
  3. If the shops lose customers, some shops will close.
  4. If the market uses the empty car park, the street will be quiet.
  5. If you live near the market, you will hear the noise.
  6. The sellers will keep their prices low if they do not pay for a shop.

Look and talk to your partner

  1. What is the verb form after if?
    the present simple: opens, are, lose, uses, live, do not pay
  2. What is the verb form in the other part?
    will + verb
  3. Is there a market on Cross Road now?
    No – it is only a possibility. Maybe yes, maybe no.
  4. Where is if in sentence 6? Is there a comma?
    In the middle – no comma.

SAY: “Peter said the buses will be late. Let us think about what will happen if a market opens. Read the six sentences with your partner. Which part has the idea that might happen? Which part is the result?” DO: read sentences 1–3 aloud with the class, then pairs read 4–6. Ask the four questions in order; a learner answers, THEN click (one answer per click). Do not teach the rule yet – let them notice it. Learners look at: the projector. 10 minutes.

Handout 5 – Task D · First conditional: if … will …

Part 1 – the conditionPart 2 – the result
If + present simple
If it rains tomorrow,		will + verb
we will stay at home.

It is possible in the future – and this is the result.

Other order – no comma: We will stay at home if it rains tomorrow.

unless = if … not
Unless you hurry, you will miss the bus.
If you do not hurry, you will miss the bus.

Check

  1. “If it rains tomorrow, I will take an umbrella.” Which part is the condition?
    If it rains tomorrow
  2. “I will take an umbrella ___ it rains tomorrow.” Is there a comma?
    No – the if part is in the middle.
  3. “Unless you pay, you will not get a ticket.” = “If you ___ pay, you will not get a ticket.”
    do not
  4. Which is wrong? A If it rains … B If it will rain …
    B – no will after if

SAY: “Part 1 is the condition: if and the present simple. Part 2 is the result: will and the verb. We use it when something is possible in the future. Today is not the future, so we do not say ‘if it will rain’ – we say ‘if it rains’.” Read the two example sentences aloud and have the class repeat. “Unless means if not.” DO: ask the four check questions; a learner answers, THEN click. Give out Handout 5 (the rule is on it, so nobody needs to copy). Learners look at: the projector and Handout 5. 10 minutes.
