Finish the sentences – say them to your partner

  1. I like living in London because …
  2. It was cold, so …
  3. Although I was tired, …
  4. Buses in London are cheap. However, …
  5. I came to college today because …
  6. The weather was bad, so …
  7. Although English is difficult, …
  8. My friend lives far away. However, …

Four small words

because gives a reason.

so gives a result.

although and however show a different idea.

Although … (one sentence) · However, … (new sentence, comma after however)

👀 Look at the board
Learners: projector, then their partner
SAY: “Finish each sentence with your own idea. Say it to your partner, then swap.” Model number 1 (“I like living in London because there is a lot to do.”). DO: pairs say all eight aloud; take three answers. Listen for: a reason after because; a result after so; a comma after However. Learners: look at the board, talk to a partner. 15 minutes.

Two letters to the Local Voice

The newspaper asks: Should Cross Road have a Saturday street market?

Amina Hassan says yes. Peter Green says no.

  1. Your teacher gives you Letter A or Letter B.
  2. Read your letter once. Do not show it to anyone.
  3. Answer the 6 questions on your sheet.
  4. Then find a partner who read the other letter.

Main idea and reasons

The main idea is what the whole letter is about. It is often in the first sentence.

A reason tells you why the writer thinks this. Look for because and so.

Exam tip: the questions use different words from the text. Find the part of the text that means the same.

👀 Look at the board
Learners: their own handout (3A or 3B); the board only for instructions
SAY: “Today you read a letter to a newspaper. Some of you have Letter A, some have Letter B. Read your own letter and answer the six questions on your sheet. You have about 25 minutes.” DO: count off A and B round the room; give each learner only their own letter (Handout 3A or 3B). Walk round; do not explain words yet. Fast finishers: the word search (button at the top). Learners: look at their own handout.

WHOLE CLASS – eyes on the board

Share your letter – then compare

  1. Find a partner with the other letter.
  2. Tell your partner: What does the writer want? Why? Do not read your letter aloud.
  3. Fill in the table on Handout 4.
  4. Write four sentences with because, so, although, however.
  5. Tell your partner: I agree with … because …

Model – row 1

Amina wants a Saturday market on Cross Road.

Peter does not want a market on Cross Road.

Useful

Amina thinks … because …
Peter says … so …
Although Amina …, Peter …
Peter says … However, …

👀 Look at the board
Learners: Handout 4 and a partner; the board for the model
SAY: “Find someone with the other letter. Tell them in your own words what the writer wants and why – do not read it out. Then fill in the table.” DO: model row 1 above. Pairs of A and B (a group of three is fine). Walk round: listen for because/so/although/however used correctly; do not correct, ask “Reason, result or different idea?” Finish: hear three ‘I agree with … because …’. Check: table row 1 – Amina wants a Saturday market; Peter does not. Reasons: Amina – busy people can shop on Saturday; sellers do not pay for a shop; neighbours meet. Peter – street too narrow, buses late; shops lose customers; rubbish. Learners: look at Handout 4. 20 minutes.
