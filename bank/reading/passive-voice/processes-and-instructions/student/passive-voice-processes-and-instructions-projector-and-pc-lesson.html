Warm-up · How is a cup of tea made?

Active

A person makes tea.

The baker makes the bread.

Someone delivers the letters.

Passive

Tea is made.

The bread is made by the baker.

The letters are delivered.

is / are + a past participle (made, delivered, cleaned) for now · was / were + a past participle for the past

Tell your partner how tea is made. Use: First · Next · Then · Finally
the water is boiled · the tea bag is put in a cup · the water is poured · the tea bag is taken out

BEFORE: post this page’s link in Teams General and log the PCs in. SAY: “Log in. While we wait, look at the board. In the left box a person does the action. In the right box we do not say who. We say what happens to the thing: Tea is made.” POINT: is / are + made, delivered. ASK: “What is the same in the right box?” (is or are, then a word like made). SAY: “How is a cup of tea made? Tell your partner. Start with First. The water is boiled …” MODEL the first two steps. DO: pairs say the whole process (4 minutes); hear two pairs. Late arrivals join a pair. Learners look at: the projector, then their partner. 10 minutes.

Task B · Read the process and answer – the point counts only if your first click is right

The questions and answers use different words from the text. Find the part of the text with the same meaning.

How bread is made at the Broadway Bakery

1 Every morning at 4 o’clock, the bakers start work. First, flour, water, salt and yeast are put into a big machine, and the mixture is mixed for ten minutes. The mixture is called dough.

2 Next, the dough is left in a warm place for one hour. During this time, it rises and becomes much bigger.

3 After that, the dough is cut into pieces, and each piece is shaped by hand. The shaped pieces are placed in metal tins.

4 Then the tins are put into a very hot oven. The bread is baked for about thirty minutes, until it is brown.

5 Finally, the bread is taken out of the oven and left to cool. At 7 o’clock it is delivered to cafés and shops in Hackney by bicycle, so customers can buy it fresh.

Task C · Active or passive?

In an active sentence the person or thing that does the action comes first. In a passive sentence the action happens to the thing: is / are / was / were + past participle. Click the answer. Exam skill: recognising passives.

Learners’ code for page 4: 0000 – say it or write it on the board when the class moves on. SAY: “Page 4. Is the sentence active or passive? Look for is, are, was or were and a past participle.” MODEL number 1: “The baker makes the bread. The baker does the action – active.” DO: pairs may talk. CHECK: Show next answer one per click; ASK: “What are the two parts of the passive?” (be + past participle). For each passive ask: “Who did it?” – not always said. Answers: 1 active 2 passive 3 passive 4 active 5 passive 6 active 7 passive 8 active 9 passive 10 active. Learners look at: page 4 on their own PC.

Task D · Read the instructions and answer – the point counts only if your first click is right

Instructions are in order: step 1, step 2, step 3 … Read them in the same order, and look at the words before, then and after.

Renew your library book online

Before you start you need your library card number and your PIN.

1 Go to the library website and click “My account”.

2 Type your card number and your PIN. Then click “Log in”.

3 Click “My loans”. A list of your books is shown.

4 Tick the book you want to keep. Then click “Renew”.

5 An email is sent to you. It shows the new date for returning the book. Keep it.

Notes A book can be renewed up to three times. A book cannot be renewed if another reader has asked for it. A small charge is made for late books.

Learners’ code for page 5: 0000 – say it or write it on the board when the class moves on. SAY: “Page 5. This is a notice from a library: instructions. Read the steps in order. Then answer the eight questions. 15 minutes.” DO: ‘PIN’ is a secret number; ‘renew’ means keep the book for a longer time – say this only if a learner asks. Instructions use commands (Click, Tick, Type). The passives are in the sentences about what the website does (is shown, is sent, is made). CHECK: Show next answer one per click; a learner reads the words that prove it. ASK once: “Which sentences are passive?” (A list of your books is shown. An email is sent to you. A small charge is made …). Answers: 1 your card number and your PIN 2 My account 3 click “Log in” 4 a list of your books 5 tick the book you want to keep 6 the new date for returning the book 7 up to three times 8 Another reader has asked for it. Task E · Describe a process in Teams

Choose one process

  1. How rice is cooked
  2. How a letter is posted and delivered
  3. How a cup of tea or coffee is made
  4. How something is made in your country (food, clothes, …)

Write your answer in Teams (General): five sentences.

Use

First, … Next, … Then, … Finally, …

Use is / are + past participle at least four times.

Help: washed · boiled · put · poured · cooked · cut · posted · collected · delivered · left · served

Check before you post: capital letter · full stop · is / are + a past participle (made, not make)

Then read two classmates’ posts and reply to one: I like your process because …

Learners’ code for page 6: 0000 – say it or write it on the board when the class moves on. SAY: “Page 6. Open Teams, General. Choose one process and describe it in five sentences. Use First, Next, Then and Finally. Use the passive – is or are and a past participle – at least four times. Then read two posts and reply to one.” DO: walk round; point at a wrong form and ask “Is the thing doing the action, or is it happening to the thing? Which verb form?” – do not correct it for them. Collect two good posts to read aloud on the next page. If short of time: four sentences, no reply. Learners look at: Teams General on their own PC. 20 minutes.
