Prove it · Task 1 · Transport Research Results

Give the answer – then read the words in the text that prove it.

  1. True or false? False – “Travellers … feel the train fares are too high”
  2. Two reasons (need both) any two: houses are cheaper · travel is cheaper · living expenses are cheaper (in the north)
  3. Who are ‘they’? C Companies – “Some companies are moving there because they pay lower rent”
  4. ‘Questionnaire’ means… B a list of questions
  5. The purpose of the text C reports – “Transport Research Results”: it tells us what a survey found
  6. Two reasons (need both) they pay lower rent · they get staff more easily
  7. Alphabetical order fare · film · music · podcast

One mark each. Question 2 and question 6 need both reasons.

SAY: “Stop writing. Swap papers with your partner. Take a different colour pen. I ask for the answer. Then a different learner reads the words in the text that prove it.” DO: for each question – one learner answers, another reads the proof, THEN click Show next answer (one answer per click). ASK for 3: “Who is ‘they’? Singular or plural? Person or thing?” ASK for 5: “Which wrong answer was tempting? Why is it wrong?” Learners look at: the paper they are marking.

Prove it · Task 2 · Ali Khan’s letter and the reply

Give the answer – then read the words in the text that prove it.

  1. Why is Ali writing? B wanting a job – “I would like to apply for the job of a postman”
  2. Where does Ali live now? Leeds – “when I moved to Leeds … I work in a newsagent shop at the moment”
  3. Who is ‘I’ in Text B? C James Turner – he signs Text B (“I” = the writer)
  4. True or false? False – “similar” means nearly the same
  5. The purpose of Text B A offer an interview – “I have arranged an interview for you”
  6. Who must Ali contact? Debbie Seymour – “contact Debbie Seymour by email”
  7. Alphabetical order – which word is last? attend – accounts · apply · arranged · attend

DO: the same routine – answer, proof, then click. ASK for 10: “Who is ‘I’? Look at the name at the end of the letter.” ASK for 12: “What is the purpose? Which words tell you?” Learners look at: the paper they are marking.

Prove it · Task 3 · What is your favourite fruit?

Give the answer – then read the words in the text that prove it.

  1. True or false? False – it is about mangoes in general: where they come from, size, health benefits, how to prepare them
  2. What colour before it is ready to eat? green – “The skin … is green before it is ready to eat”
  3. Bullet points are used to… C give a list – the three health benefits
  4. ‘Delicious’ means… A tasty – “Mangoes are delicious and most people enjoy them”
  5. This text… C informs – it gives facts about mangoes
  6. Two health advantages (need both) any two: improve eye health · protect against some cancers · help to control blood pressure
  7. Which list is in alphabetical order? B flavour · fresh · season · smooth (fl before fr; se before sm)

Marks out of 21. Question 20 needs both advantages.

DO: the same routine. ASK for 15: “Is the text only about growing mangoes? What else is it about?” ASK for 17: “Why are there bullet points? What do they show?” ASK for 21: “flavour, fresh – which comes first? Look at the second letter. season, smooth – look at the second letter.” SAY: “Count the marks out of 21. Write the number at the top. Give the paper back.” DO: hands up for each question; type the class’s two weakest question numbers on the next page. Learners look at: the Task 3 sheet they are marking.

Fix it – and your target

  1. Look at the questions you got wrong. Choose two.
  2. Write the correct answer in the margin.
  3. Write the words in the text that prove it.
  4. Tell your partner: why was the wrong answer tempting?
  5. Write at the top: My score: __ / 21. Next time I will …

Our two hardest questions

Which words in the question are different from the words in the text?

SAY: “Look at the questions you got wrong. Choose two. For each one, write the correct answer and the words in the text that prove it. Then tell your partner why the wrong answer was tempting.” DO: type the class’s two weakest question numbers in the boxes. Read the proof sentence for the first one aloud and ASK: “Which words in the question are different from the words in the text?” FINISH: “Write your score out of 21 and one target: Next time I will …” Hear three targets. If short of time: one correction each. Learners look at: their own paper.
